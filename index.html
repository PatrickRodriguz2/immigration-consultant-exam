Built to the published blueprint

The exam has 135 multiple-choice questions in three hours: 125 are scored and 10 are unscored pilot questions. The College's Candidate Guide publishes each competency unit's share of the scored questions, and on a 125-question paper every share is a whole number. A full mock paper is sampled to exactly those counts, with 10 unscored pilot questions placed at random.

Inside each unit the app groups the competencies into 43 study areas. That finer split, and its weights, are the app's own — the Candidate Guide publishes the units only.

A target, not a pass mark

The College sets the pass mark for each sitting and does not publish it, so this app never pretends to know it. You see a projected score out of 125 and set your own target (75% by default); every mock paper tells you how far above or below your target you landed, unit by unit.

What is inside

  • 1,377 original questions across the 43 study areas, each with an explanation and a pinpoint reference to the Act, regulation, rule, guideline or case it turns on.
  • Case sets — a short client situation with three to five questions on the same facts, as on the real exam.
  • Full and half mock papers — 135 questions in three hours, or 68 in ninety minutes — with flag-for-review and a question grid.
  • Practitioner drills, generated fresh each time with worked answers: the permanent-resident residency obligation, citizenship physical presence, IRB and Federal Court deadlines, and sponsorship undertaking periods.
  • A generated client travel file with questions worked from it.
  • A Library of study-area and quick-reference cards.
  • Spaced repetition, and it all works offline. No account, no sign-in, no data collected.

Where the content comes from

Written from the primary law, read rather than recalled: the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act and Regulations, the Citizenship Act and Regulations, the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants Act and Regulations, the Code of Professional Conduct, the rules of the four Immigration and Refugee Board divisions and the Chairperson's Guidelines, the Federal Courts rules and the leading cases — plus the Candidate Guide and the Essential Competencies the College publishes for candidates. The law is as of September 2026, for the October 2026 and March 2027 sittings. No commercial prep product and no actual exam question was used.

Exam practice, not advice. Every question is about a fictional client. Immigration law and policy change often: always confirm current law and requirements with official sources.
